
\chapter{Perks, Abilities, and Classes}
These are rules that modify the capabilities of characters,
and can be added to a character who meets the W.I.Z.A.R.D.S. requirements when they level-up.

\vspace{0.4em}

Unless another perk is mentioned as a prerequisite, 
a character only needs to meet the W.I.Z.A.R.D.S. requirements of a perk or ability
in order to add it when they level-up.

\begin{DesignerNote}
  Additional perks, abilities, and classes may be introduced specific to a campaign/setting, 
  but those in this book are designed to be used by characters in any campaign.
\end{DesignerNote}




\section{Perks}
\textit{Perks} are passive effects that apply to a character at all times.

\vspace{0.4em}

The table of perks can be found in Appendix A: Perks.


\subsection{Skills}
\textit{Skills} are a variety of downtime abilities that can be practiced 
whenever a character levels-up in a Town.

\vspace{0.4em}

A subset of perks enable characters to use these skills, 
or else modify the rules of the skill for that character.

\vspace{0.4em}

The rules for using the various skills can be found in the next chapter, Chapter 6: Skills.

\vspace{0.4em}

The list of perks that enable skills can be found under Skills \& Professions in Appendix A: Perks.


\newpage


\section{Abilities}

\textit{Abilities} provide an effect that the character may activate as an Action.

\vspace{0.4em}

Abilities available to all characters are found under Core Abilities in Chapter 3: Combat,
and additional abilities can be found in Appendix B: Abilities.


\subsection{Spells}
Spells are a subtype of ability that are used via the \textbf{Cast Spell} core ability.
These require a mana focus and have a Mana cost that must be paid.

\vspace{0.4em}

There are three archetypes of spell: \textit{Destruction}, \textit{Restoration}, and \textit{Thaumaturgy}.

\vspace{0.4em}

The table of basic spells can be found in Appendix D: Spells.




\section{Character Classes}
\textit{Character Classes} are collections of perks and abilities that represent certain 
archetypal playstyles.

\vspace{0.4em}

Whenever a character could add a perk or ability, they may choose one from among the character classes. 

\vspace{0.4em}

Classes often form a prerequisite tree where one or more early perks are required to enter the class.

\vspace{0.4em}

Classes can be found in Appendix C: Character Classes.
