

\chapter{Adventures}

\section{Questlines}
\textit{Questlines} are structured sub-components of an overall campaign narrative.

\vspace{0.4em}

Questlines take characters to various \textit{Locations}, 
offer various \textit{Rewards} for their completion, 
and are punctuated by \textit{Downtime} in Towns.


\section{Locations}
Adventure \textit{Locations} are the places where planned encounters occur, 
specifically those that serve the advancement of the questline's narrative plot.

\vspace{0.4em}

Adventure locations are typically distinguished from settlements, but may take place in towns,
as the narrative requires, or they may be abandonded structures or ruins,
or geographical places such as mountain caves like mountain caves, forest glades, 
hilltops and swamps.

\vspace{0.4em}

Their layout serves the gameplay of their associated missions, 
and may be constructed around combat, puzzles, and mazes.
They may feature progressive encounters with a series of rooms, hallways, and waves of enemies,
and typically culminate in some kind of final encounter, such as a ``boss'' or final wave of enemy combatants.

\vspace{0.4em}

A questline may take the party to one or more such locations, depending on the story the quest tells.


\newpage

\section{Rewards}
\textit{Rewards} are the items, currency, materials, or opportunities that serve as an incentive 
for the party's efforts, by providing character, party, or plot advancement.

\vspace{0.4em}

Rewards may come in the form of loot from creatures and bosses, treasure chests or vaults,
or may be given by the quest-giving NPC after completion of the questline.

\vspace{0.4em}

These should generally be proportionate to risk, within believability, 
appropriate to the challenge that merited the reward.


\section{Downtime}
Adventures are punctuated by \textit{Downtime}, spent in Towns, 
where characters are given an opportunity to level-up and,
in towns with the appropriate amenities, to engage in activities provided by their skills,
such as crafting or enchanting, etc.

\vspace{0.4em}

When characters have downtime, they restore Life and Mana up to their default maximum.
Spells and Abilities may be used as part of downtime activities (i.e., skills) 
without paying their associated Life or Mana costs, 
but any variable costs (i.e., costs with X in them) must use a value that is 
less than the character's maximum reservoir for that cost.
