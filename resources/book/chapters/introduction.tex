
\chapter{Introduction}


\section{What is W.I.Z.A.R.D.S.?}
The W.I.Z.A.R.D.S. System is a tabletop roleplaying framework 
that can be adapted to a variety of settings and game formats. 

\vspace{0.4em}

The purpose of this rulebook is to describe a 
simple and perennial gameplay experience that
eliminates the need to monitor various ever-changing rulesets 
in order to enjoy tabletop roleplaying and wargaming,
while providing mechanical depth that supports a wide 
variety of niche character types and game scenarios.

\vspace{0.4em}

It is named after its Character Attribute system, 
which uses the memorable acronym, ``W.I.Z.A.R.D.S."
This attribute system is explored in Part II of this book.



\section{Player Responsibilities}
In W.I.Z.A.R.D.S., everyone at the table is a \textit{Player}. 

\vspace{0.4em}

Players are responsible for creating and controlling one or more characters,
determining their actions and rolling dice to determine their 
successes and failures.

\vspace{0.4em}

In an adventuring campaign setting, 
where the players may interact with a world populated by characters,
a player may assume the role of \textit{Game Master} (GM)
in order to control the actions of the world's antagonists.

Although this role has enhanced responsibilities in the game, 
from the perspective of the rules, 
the GM is generally treated as ``just another player."

\vspace{0.4em}

This system is rules-driven and designed to remove as much 
ambiguity as possible,
so that players and DMs are never overburdened by 
decisions or interpretations.



\section{Game Types}
W.I.Z.A.R.D.S. is designed for tabletop games with combat using miniatures, 
and some rules may rely on concepts like ``Line-of-Sight" and ``Base Contact" 
that may not translate well to theater-of-mind approaches to combat.

\vspace{0.4em}

While this system was conceived for use in tabletop roleplaying games (TTRPGs),
its flexible architecture supports a variety of game formats including 
squadron-based missions and even full-scale wargaming,
with or without a GM.



\section{Using this Book}
This book presumes the reader's familiarity with basic TTRPG concepts,
and is directed primarily at users who are looking for a more consistent 
and versatile system for roleplaying and wargaming that can be used
to replace a variety of systems with a single, universal ruleset.
However, W.I.Z.A.R.D.S. is designed to be easy to teach and to learn,
so new users should also be able to quickly pick up these concepts 
while playing with more experienced players.

\vspace{0.4em}

The following chapters describe a limited set of core rules that convey
the essential principles of W.I.Z.A.R.D.S. 
The rules described in this book can be extended to support 
unique campaign settings and scenarios
by using existing mechanics as inspiration
in order to preserve game balance.

\subsection{Part I: Core Rules}
The first part describes the core mechanics that are available to 
every character, and every player should commit these rules to memory.

\subsection{Part II: Characters}
The second part contains rules regarding characters, 
including core perks, abilities, and skills that players can use to 
build their characters and give them a unique playstyle and identity.
Players should become familiar with the process for creating characters,
but otherwise the only rules they will need to know are the rules they choose
while building their character.

\subsection{Part III: Equipment \& Part IV: Magic}
Similarly, the third and fourth parts contain rules that pertain to
specific character builds, which only need to be remembered by players
who use them in their character(s),
and which otherwise may be referred to as they become needed. 
These parts describe equipment and magic, respectively.

\subsection{Part V: Campaigns}
The fifth part contains rules and guidance for creating worlds and settings,
and is directed primarily towards GMs and campaign creators.
