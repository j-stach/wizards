## Attacks
- target in range, attack ability
- clash
- damage

### Formation
- Similarly-sized Characters continuously within 1" can be grouped in a formation
- If their movement is the same, they can be moved in a group
- Attacking: If multiple grouped characters would make identical projectile attack rolls 
against a group in common range, instead the rolls can be combined into a single Clash roll
that is rolled as a pool.
In this case, the group that is targeted rolls the same number of d6,
and the dice are paired in descending order, then the clashes are resolved normally, 
with each side adding the lowest modifier of characters in the group.
The defending player assigns unsaved attacks as they choose among the members of the group,
then damage is rolled for each unsaved attack.

### Flanking
When an attack deals damage to a target, all characters that are within range to attack the target,
and can draw a straight line from their base, through the enemy model, 
to the base of the ally that was source of the damage, 
gain *Flanking* against the target until the start of their next turn.

While a character is Flanking a target, 
they gain Advantage on Clash rolls for attacks against that target.

### High Ground
Two characters at different elevations of sheer (vertical) terrain are considered in base contact if:
- the separation between bases is less than 1" (*tbd: based on size?*)
- the characters' bases are in contact with the ledge and foot of the terrain

### Cover
Projectile attacks with partially-obscured Line-of-Sight grant *Cover* to the target during the attack.
Attacks against enemies with Cover lose Advantage on Clash rolls.


## Movement & Terrain
- Can move up to move distance 
- Designate movement path
- If it would intersect with hostile bases, you trigger reactions mid-movement,
- Can move vertically at half-speed, but ending movement on a vertical surface drops you to the bottom

### Hazards
When your path of movement crosses or ends in hazardous terrain, 
roll on the Table (d10) for the terrain effect.
